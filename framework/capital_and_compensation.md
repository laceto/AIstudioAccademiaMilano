# Capital and Compensation Framework — AI Studio Accademia Milano

Operational financial framework governing how AI Studio generates, allocates, and protects capital, and how the founder is compensated for risk and execution.

**Owner**: Marco
**Status**: active

---

## Foundational Rule

The founder must be compensated.

Luigi absorbs uncertainty, execution pressure, financial exposure, reputational exposure, and technological experimentation simultaneously. Compensation is not a reward for success — it is a requirement for sustainability. An underpaid founder eventually stops building. This company does not operate as a hobby.

---

## Anti-Hobby Principle

AI Studio is an economic machine, a production engine, and a value generation system.

Any decision that treats it as a research project, an experimental toy, or a non-commercial operation is a strategic error. Every hour of work must have a line of sight to revenue, asset value, or strategic leverage. When it does not, that work should be deprioritized or eliminated.

---

## Revenue Philosophy

Revenue should progressively shift from one-time project work toward:

1. **Recurring subscriptions** — clients paying monthly for ongoing AI operations, monitoring, or content systems.
2. **Retained AI operations** — clients retaining AI Studio to run workflows, automations, or agent pipelines on their behalf.
3. **Reusable templates** — packaged solutions sold multiple times with minimal marginal effort.
4. **Deployable infrastructure** — SaaS products, APIs, and tools that generate revenue while Luigi builds other things.
5. **Scalable IP** — codebases, workflows, and systems that appreciate in value over time.

One-time project fees are acceptable in the early phase but are not the target steady state. Every project should be evaluated for its recurring revenue conversion potential.

## Project Pricing Logic

Marco applies the following framework when pricing any new project.

### Step 1 — Cost Floor

Calculate the minimum acceptable price:

```
Cost Floor = (Estimated hours × Luigi's hourly rate floor)
           + (Estimated infrastructure costs × 1.5 safety margin)
           + (Third-party API costs at projected usage)
           + (Risk buffer: 20% of subtotal for unknowns)
```

Luigi's hourly rate floor: set at a level that sustains operations. Marco maintains the current rate in a private working document reviewed quarterly.

### Step 2 — Value Ceiling

Estimate the maximum defensible price based on:
- Time the client saves per month × 12 months (annualized value).
- Revenue the system is expected to generate for the client.
- Comparable market rates for similar software from traditional agencies.

Take 10–25% of annualized client value as the fee range, depending on project complexity and relationship stage.

### Step 3 — Positioning

Land in the range between Cost Floor and Value Ceiling. Default to the upper third of that range for new clients with no prior relationship. Offer a lower entry price only if it creates a clear path to recurring revenue.

### Pricing Tiers

| Project Type                        | Price Range (EUR) | Payment Terms        |
|-------------------------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Simple automation or chatbot        | 500–1,500         | 100% upfront         |
| Full web app or AI workflow         | 1,500–5,000       | 50% deposit, 50% on delivery |
| Complex system or multi-agent build | 5,000–15,000      | 40% deposit, 30% mid, 30% delivery |
| Recurring AI operations retainer    | 500–2,000/month   | Monthly subscription, 3-month minimum |
| SaaS product (template license)     | 200–800 per seat  | Upfront or annual    |

These are floor figures. Adjust upward based on client size, urgency, and industry margin.

## Capital Allocation Priorities

When revenue arrives, Marco allocates it in the following priority order. This order is not negotiable — it is the operating discipline of the company.

### Priority 1 — Infrastructure

Infrastructure is the engine. It must always be funded.

Covers:
- Cloud compute (VPS, serverless, GPU as needed)
- API access (OpenAI, Anthropic, data providers)
- Deployment platforms (hosting, CI/CD, monitoring)
- Domain names, SSL, storage
- Software tools and licenses

Target allocation: 15–25% of monthly revenue, or a fixed monthly cap reviewed quarterly. Infrastructure costs above this threshold require Luigi sign-off.

### Priority 2 — Founder Sustainability

Luigi's operational continuity comes second. This is not a luxury — it is a business risk. A founder who cannot cover living costs makes poor strategic decisions.

Covers:
- Monthly living expenses (rent, food, transport)
- Health and personal stability
- Any tools or equipment required for personal productivity

Target allocation: enough to maintain baseline stability. The specific figure is a private threshold maintained by Marco and reviewed when revenue changes materially.

### Priority 3 — Asset Generation

Reinvestment into IP that compounds over time.

Covers:
- Building reusable templates and selling them.
- Developing automation pipelines that can be sold to multiple clients.
- Creating AI agent configurations that reduce per-project build time.
- Producing educational content that builds long-term inbound authority.

Target allocation: 10–20% of monthly profit after Priority 1 and 2 are funded.

### Priority 4 — Expansion

Physical and community scale-up, funded only from surplus.

Covers:
- Physical atelier space setup (workstations, screens, AI terminals, QR walls)
- Workshop materials and event hosting
- Community events and demo days in Milan
- Any marketing spend

Target allocation: funded from profit above a defined monthly floor. Expansion is never funded by taking on debt or reducing Priority 1–3 allocations.

This table is a starting model. Marco adjusts allocations quarterly based on actual revenue, costs, and strategic priorities.

---

## Operating Reserve

AI Studio must maintain an operating reserve equivalent to 3 months of infrastructure + founder sustainability costs at all times.

This reserve is not investment capital. It is not spent on expansion. It is the buffer that allows Luigi to say no to bad clients, take on ambitious projects without cash pressure, and survive slow months without making poor decisions.

When the reserve is below target, expansion spending stops immediately and the surplus is redirected to rebuilding it.

## Financial Risk Flags

Marco monitors for the following conditions and escalates to Luigi when they arise:

| Risk Condition                              | Threshold for Escalation                         |
|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Infrastructure cost overrun                 | Monthly infra spend exceeds budget by >20%       |
| Project underpricing                        | Project cost exceeds quoted price by >15%        |
| Client concentration                        | Single client exceeds 40% of active revenue      |
| Unpaid invoices                             | Invoice outstanding >14 days past due date       |
| Operating reserve depletion                 | Reserve falls below 2 months of fixed costs      |
| Recurring revenue below target              | Recurring revenue <30% of total monthly revenue  |

All escalations are logged and reviewed with Luigi before the end of the week in which they arise.

## Long-Term Financial Model

The target steady state is:

```
One human (Luigi)
+ AI agents (Stacy, Gianni, Chiara, Francesca, Marco + Risk Agents)
+ software infrastructure (deployed products, automations, templates)
+ risk management (Financial Controller monitoring all positions)
=
High-leverage economic organism generating output comparable to a traditional 5-person software team
at a fraction of the cost.
```

The path from current state to that model runs through:

1. Consistent project delivery (builds operational cash flow).
2. Template and IP creation (reduces per-project effort over time).
3. Recurring retainer clients (stabilizes cash flow).
4. SaaS product launches (creates non-linear revenue).
5. Physical expansion (creates local monopoly on AI implementation in Milan).

Marco's role is to ensure the financial conditions exist for each of these stages to happen in order.
